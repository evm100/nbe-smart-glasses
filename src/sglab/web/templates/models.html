How to read these results
MAE
Mean absolute error: how far off a prediction is on average, in mmHg, ignoring whether it is too high or too low. Lower is better. A cuff itself is typically within ~5 mmHg of an arterial line.
Baseline
The simple guess the model has to beat. Calibrated runs (the default): “BP stays at the session's first cuff reading”. Otherwise: “everyone has the average BP”. A model that doesn't beat it has learned nothing useful, however small its MAE looks.
Bias ± SD
Average signed error (does it read high or low?) and how much the errors scatter. The AAMI standard for BP devices asks for bias within ±5 and SD ≤ 8 mmHg, on 85+ people.
r change
Does the predicted change from the calibration go up and down with the measured one? 1 = follows perfectly, 0 = no relation. This is the number to watch for cold-pressor / exercise sessions.
Folds
Each prediction comes from a model trained without that subject (or that session, while there is only one subject). That is what makes the scores honest: a model always looks great on the data it was fitted to.
Holdout
Subjects locked away from all training and tuning (holdout_subjects in the settings), scored once at the end. Worth setting once there are ~10 subjects.
Sweep
Tries many model settings and feature sets and keeps the best. The best of many always looks a bit lucky, so a sweep also reports a nested score: the whole search repeated without each subject, its winner scored on them. Trust the nested number.
