Run a recording

  1. Open Home and check Ready to record? — everything should have a green tick.
  2. Switch the devices on and put them on the subject. Wait until they show online on Devices (about 15 s after power-on).
  3. Press New trial. Tick the devices, choose the subject (or “new subject” — a code like S005, never a name), the protocol and posture. Press Start recording.
  4. On the live page, look at the top row first: a heart rate should appear within ~15 s and Signal quality should say Good or Fair. If it says Poor, re-seat the sensor now — see poor signal.
  5. During the recording, log what happens with the buttons under Log readings & markers (cuff readings, pulse-ox, “subject moved”, your own notes). Markers are what make a recording understandable later.
  6. Press ■ Stop, answer the three questions (how it went, notes, keep?) and save. Processing starts by itself; the recording's page opens.

Nothing is lost if you make a mistake: “Keep = no” only marks a recording as discarded, the files stay.

Log cuff and pulse-ox readings

  1. Press Cuff inflation started at the moment the cuff starts pumping. (While the cuff squeezes the arm the finger signal on that arm is disturbed — the software needs to know when.)
  2. When the cuff shows its result, type systolic / diastolic (and pulse) and press Log BP.
  3. The first good cuff reading of a recording calibrates the live blood-pressure estimate. Take it while the subject is at rest and the signal quality is good.
  4. To be able to judge the model afterwards, take further readings — ideally after blood pressure has changed (exercise, cold pressor, posture).

Look at a recording

  • Open it from Sessions. The tiles at the top are the summary; every plot below shares one time axis.
  • Drag on any plot to zoom in — all plots follow, and the detail is loaded again at full resolution (down to single pulses and single camera frames). Double-click to see the whole recording. The buttons 10 min / 1 min / 10 s and ◀ ▶ do the same.
  • Click a plot to place the cursor; the eye-camera picture jumps to that moment.
  • The web address changes as you zoom — copy it into a board post to point a colleague at exactly what you see.
  • Signal quality tells you how much of the recording was usable and, in plain words, why the rest was rejected.
  • Write what you notice under Notes & discussion; it also appears on the board.

Camera glasses and the USB gateway

Camera glasses can send over Wi-Fi by themselves, or through a USB cable to your computer running the small NBE USB gateway program (an admin downloads it for a given pair of glasses from the Nodes page). While the gateway runs and the cable is in, the glasses use the cable.

  • Start the gateway, plug the glasses in, wait until the glasses show online on Devices.
  • Only one gateway may run per pair of glasses. If the glasses stay offline although your gateway runs, someone left another one running: the Devices page lists it with a shut down button.

My device is offline

Check these in order
  1. Power. Is it switched on / is the battery charged? After power-on it needs ~15 s to appear.
  2. Top-right corner of this website. If it says “devices link down”, no Wi-Fi device can be seen by anyone — that is a server problem; tell an admin.
  3. Wi-Fi. The device must be in range of the lab's device network. Move closer to the router and wait 30 s.
  4. Glasses on USB: is the gateway program running on your computer, and is it the only one? (See above.)
  5. State says “stale”: it was online and went silent (out of range or battery empty). It recovers by itself when it is back.
  6. Still offline: unplug / switch off for 5 s and on again. If that doesn't help, post a “Problem” on the board with the device's name.

Recording started but no data arrives

“no data for … s – reconnecting” on the live page

The software restarts the device's stream by itself (within 3–20 s); you don't need to stop the recording. If it doesn't come back after half a minute: check the device still has power and is in Wi-Fi range. Data a Wi-Fi device couldn't send is kept on the device for several minutes and delivered when the connection returns.

Open Stream health at the bottom of the live page for per-device detail (rate, dropped samples, the device's own report).

Signal quality is poor

The pulse trace looks ragged, or “Signal quality” says Poor
  • Contact. The sensor must touch skin, flat, with light steady pressure. Glasses: seat the temple arm snugly; hair under the sensor ruins the signal. Finger: not too tight (squeezes the blood out), not loose.
  • Movement. Talking, chewing and head movement disturb the glasses signal. Ask the subject to stay still for readings that matter, and log a marker (“talking”, “subject moved”) when they don't.
  • Cold hands give a weak finger signal — warm them first.
  • Light. Direct sunlight or a lamp shining on the sensor can saturate it.

On the recording's page, Signal quality shows how much was usable and which check rejected the rest.

The eye is not found

The camera picture says “NO EYE” or the circle sits in the wrong place
  • Look at the picture: the eye should be roughly centred and in focus. Adjust the glasses, then press Re-find eye (takes ~2 s with the eye open, looking straight ahead).
  • “washed out” = too much light on the camera; “too dark / no contrast” = too little. Change the lighting or ask an admin about the camera's exposure setting.
  • After the subject touches or shifts the glasses, press Re-find eye again and log the marker “glasses adjusted”.

No blood-pressure number

The blood-pressure tile stays at “---/--”

The tile says why underneath. The usual reasons: no cuff reading has been logged yet (the estimate needs one to calibrate — see cuff readings); too few good beats in the last 45 s (fix the signal); or no model has been trained for that sensor site yet (Models page). The number is a research estimate in validation, not a measurement — never use it for anything medical.

I closed the page / the computer slept mid-recording

The recording keeps running on the server

Open the website again: a red bar at the top says ● Recording with a link back to the live view. If the server itself was restarted, the data recorded up to then is safe; tell an admin, who can recover it (sglab recover).

Plots are empty afterwards

“Not processed yet” on a recording's page

Press Process now on that page (or Process all on Sessions). Raw signals can always be viewed immediately with the Raw counts button. If it stays “queued”, the processing computer is off — Home shows that; recordings are processed when it is back.

What the words mean

PPG / pulse signal
Light reflected by the skin, which dims a little with every heartbeat. The basis for heart rate, pulse shape and the blood-pressure estimate.
Stream
One continuous series of data from one sensor on one device (a pulse signal, a camera, a chemical sensor…).
Site
Where on the body a sensor sits: glasses (temple), finger, head.
Beat accepted / rejected
Each heartbeat found in the signal is checked (shape, timing, size). Only accepted beats are used for results.
Window check
A second, stricter check on 4-second stretches of signal. Its limits are tuned per sensor site.
Perfusion
How big the pulse is compared with the overall light level — larger means a stronger signal.
HRV (RMSSD)
How much the time between heartbeats varies, in milliseconds.
PERCLOS
Share of time the eye is closed. Rises with drowsiness.
Gaze shift
One quick eye movement to a new position. Its speed is given in camera pixels per second (not yet in degrees).
Calibration (blood pressure)
The model estimates changes; one cuff reading per recording anchors them to real numbers.
Processing
The analysis run after a recording: finds beats, measures them, checks quality. Can be repeated any time; the raw recording is never changed.
Computed on the device
Results a board calculates itself and sends instead of raw data, to save battery. See Platform.
